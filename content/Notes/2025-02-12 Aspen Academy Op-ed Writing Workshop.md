## Formulaic writing
1. Hook or lead. Capture attention, make them want to keep reading. Use engaging language. Pull them in with real stories.
2. Bottom line up front. The Nut Graf. Thesis. What's your argument?
3. Evidence. Argument. Key points of the argument. Outline your logic. Use signposting like "There are 3 reasons..."
4. Conclusion. Twist.  Don't just recap. Take it to a new level. New way of thinking of a problem. Something unexpected.
5. 600-800 words typically

## General tips
- Ensure your work is timely, how is it relevant now? 
- Editors will be more excited if it ties in to something on the front page
- Make it personal, put it in your voice
- You are allowed to be opinionated and passionate
- Short sentences are better than long ones
- Read it our loud
- Write to a specific audience, imagine it as an email to a specific person who belongs to that audience
- Strong call to action, especially if it's policy related
- Ask yourself who else cares about this

## Where to Publish
- Largest outlets do invite-only, very rare to be published from cold outreach
- Local outlets
- Trade publications - Wired, MIT Tech Review
- Substack, Aspen Institute Blog

## How to Pitch
- Who are you and why are you a good source?
- Famous co-author? lead with that. 
- Attachment and in-text both
- Follow up in 3 days
- Offer exclusive review, but this is slow


## What do editors want?
- Strong argument
- Substance, non-rhetorical
- Write for a general audience, humanise it
- Supplement existing coverage with a different perspective
- At the root of it, it should be a surprising idea or a surprising person writing it
