Non chronological notes

- Identify the community that's reading your work. Learn their code. 
- How are they talking to each other, what do they care about?
- What do they challenge:
	- Academic
	- Non-academic
- "Hey readers, I’ve read your stuff. I know what you think. I admire your contribution. However, here’s one thing you got wrong."
	- What's the difference between a **gap** and an **error**?
	- Gap model assumes that knowledge is bounded, where you can find a piece to fill in.
	- 
The objective of academic writing is NOT to: 
- reveal the insides of your head
- share your ideas
- show people what you know
- share your feelings about the world
- create "permanent" knowledge
- add to the positivistic model of knowledge creation

The objective is to:
- change what people think about something
- move the conversation forward
- Locate the problem in the world, and in the community. How does it matter to the people in the community?
- Words that signal value:
	- widely reported, 
	- commonly understood
	- 
- Words that signal instability:
	- But, However, Although, Inconsistent, Anomaly
- Identify language of continuity and language of instability 
- Generate instability intentionally - readers are looking for this
- Demonstrate that the instability/inconsistency causes the community problems, that it's non-trivial and solving it is of value to them.
- Lit reviews should **enrich the problem**
- Writing can be perceived as a solution only if readers perceive the problem
