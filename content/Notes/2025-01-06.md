Extremely cold weekend in NY. Did not go out except for street sweeping - which basically involves sitting in the car for 90 minutes pretending to be ready to move for the street sweeping vehicle but when it does come by, there is no actual room for anybody to move and the machine goes by so fast. I want to cooperate, truly appreciate the sanitation system and want it to work. The way street-sweeping is set up currently does not work, it cannot work. Should tag this human-centred system design challenge. 

Is it possible that service design is the one area where all the disciplinary knowledge of the design field could make the largest impact, but is perhaps least utilised? Well, somebody's designing these public systems, like the way the subway ticketing is set up, the way we incentivise (or de-incentivise) street parking. It appears that at a very high level, it's [influenced](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-good-practice-principles-for-public-service-design-and-delivery-in-the-digital-age_2ade500b-en.html) by policy professionals, and executed by in-house teams in the relevant  department. For example, MTA's strategy to address fare evasion is being designed by their department of Organisational Strategy, and Subway Coordination and Solutions- I learnt this from watching this [recording](https://www.youtube.com/live/rIbftBUs_1A?si=VnlRUpc6ZQSecMxQ&t=5384) of a public board meeting. So cool that MTA has a [public archive of board meetings](https://new.mta.info/transparency/board-and-committee-meetings)! 

Some of the interventions include (finally) identifying that armed(!!) policing is not an effective solution, and that Black people are [disproportionately](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/01/13/nyregion/letitia-james-fare-beating-nypd.html) apprehended for fare evasion. They are now working on new turnstile mechanisms, piloted in some subway stations and widely criticised. It's a massive collaborative effort and I don't imagine any part of this is easy. I feel like all of this could be held together by the field of system design, if only educators could step up and emphasise leadership through the study of policy, public service, economics, finance, behavioural psychology and project management. I've noticed encouraging progress in the field, but "human-centred" design is not enough, it's only the bare minimum. 

This is a broad, ongoing lament of mine, that design has comfortably positioned itself as an execution arm, waiting to be called upon and given assignments. I'm part of a minority that sees more potential. The emerging area conversation around *design leadership* also lets us down by primarily focusing on how to make more money for businesses, and how to keep your design team motivated and such.
